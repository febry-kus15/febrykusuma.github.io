PhD research draft

A working space for shaping a doctoral research direction at the intersection of urban design, spatial data and climate-responsive transformation in Southeast Asian cities.

Working draft · details to be confirmed

Working title

Designing measurable urban resilience: translating spatial evidence into climate-responsive transformation for rapidly changing Southeast Asian cities.

StatusConcept development
GeographySoutheast Asia
DisciplineUrban design & spatial planning

Why this matters

Urban plans often contain strong ambitions for resilience, inclusion and low-carbon growth, but the connection between those ambitions and measurable spatial outcomes can remain unclear.

This research would explore how spatial analysis and design frameworks can work together to make those outcomes more visible, testable and useful in decision-making.

Research objectives

  • Identify spatial indicators that help describe climate-responsive urban transformation.
  • Test how GIS-based evidence can inform design choices at city, corridor and neighbourhood scales.
  • Develop a practical framework for translating analysis into implementable urban strategies.

Potential methods

  • Literature and policy review across selected Southeast Asian cities.
  • Comparative spatial analysis using open geospatial and remote-sensing data.
  • Case-study design testing with scenario mapping and stakeholder input.
  • Evaluation of the framework through measurable spatial criteria.

Possible contribution

A design-led research framework that helps practitioners connect spatial evidence, climate objectives and urban form. The intended result is useful both as an academic contribution and as a repeatable workflow for planning practice.

Next questions

  • Which climate and urban performance outcomes should the research prioritise?
  • What scale best connects city-wide policy with neighbourhood-level design decisions?
  • Which case studies offer the clearest comparison and strongest access to reliable data?
  • How can the research remain rigorous without losing practical value for designers and planners?
